# Claims file: how it works

Reference for the `draft` skill. `docs/claims-by-source.md` has one `## <Author> et al. (<year>)` section per source, each claim as a checkbox line with a section tag in brackets, and a final section that registers the author's own inferences.

## Generated quote lines

The `    - > sentence` lines under each claim are generated by `python -I scripts/claim_quotes.py --write` and never edited by hand; a hand edit is overwritten on the next run. The script also puts ✅ in front of a section title once all its boxes are ticked. Without `--write` it is a dry run that prints the `main.md` line number beside each quote; `--check` prints only whether the file is out of date; `--only <source>` limits the quote update to one source.

The quote lines carry no line numbers on purpose: when they did, every split paragraph or new heading changed the numbers throughout the file (over twelve commits, 1152 of 1261 changed lines were line numbers alone). Now a quote line changes only when its sentence does, so `git diff docs/claims-by-source.md` shows exactly which claims moved.

## Several claims in one section: `{q: <regex>}`

By default a claim is given every sentence of its section that cites the source, so claims sharing a section tag show the same sentences. Where a section holds several claims about one source, end each claim line with `{q: <regex>}` naming a distinctive phrase of its sentence (`{q: 7–12 min}`, `{q: recorded under external pacing}`). The regex replaces the citation test rather than narrowing it, so it also reaches a sentence that continues a citation without naming the source ("Both evaluated their models on small test sets ..."). Two claims may share a sentence. Leave the marker off only when a section has a single claim for that source. The script reports a regex that matches nothing, and a sentence no claim lists once any claim of the section is narrowed.

## Sources and section headings

A `## ` section not in the `SOURCES` table of `claim_quotes.py` gets a pattern from the surname in its title, and the Notes say so. Add an explicit entry when the surname is not enough: the source is cited somewhere without it (a Figure 1 timeline label), or two sections share a surname, which the script refuses to guess at and reports.

Every heading of `main.md` belongs in the `SECTION_TAG` table. A heading missing from it becomes a tag of its own and takes the sentences under it away from the tag the claims carry, so renaming a section, or promoting a bold run-in label to a `##` heading, empties every claim of that section at once. The Notes name such a heading before the claims that went empty because of it.

**Read the Notes after every run.** Both failure modes produce nothing rather than an error, so a source or a section whose claims collected no sentences shows up only there. After a structural edit of `main.md`, run the script without `--write` first, read the Notes, then write.

## Register of the author's own inferences

The last section is the only record of the statements in `main.md` that no single source supports: generalisations over several studies, reasoned steps beyond what any source says, and the author's hypotheses. `main.md` does not mark them in the text. Each item gives the wording in `main.md` (a **Text:** line), the sources it builds on and a **Support** value: `sourced`, `thin`, `inference`, `opinion`, `none` or `inherited`. The reading guide at the top of that section defines what each value claims and how it fails, and is the authority. Do not invent a seventh value: add it to the guide first, then to `SUPPORT_VALUES` in `scripts/check.py`, which warns about an unknown or missing value.

The register quotes `main.md` by hand (the **Text:** lines and the highlights under item 0), and `claim_quotes.py` reports a quoted wording that `main.md` no longer contains. When a wording changes, update the quote and keep the old wording in parentheses, which the check skips as history.
