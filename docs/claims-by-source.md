Verify each claim in the local file named under the source (all files are in `raw/`; page and figure pointers are given where known). "Checked" below means checked against the full text during the 2026-10-18 session; "from the wiki page" means the claim was written from the ingest page (an earlier session) and has not been re-checked since, so those need your closest look.

## Altrocchi et al. (2023)

Local file: `raw/altrocchi-2023-chronic.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/altrocchi-2023-chronic.md`. Status: from the wiki page.

Claims: (1) [FPDc] the study reports rate-corrected FPD with the Fridericia formula (listed among the studies that use it). (2) [High-throughput] it used 48-well MEA plates to evaluate acute (<2 h) and delayed (≥24 h) electrophysiological and cytotoxic effects of reference compounds, including tyrosine-kinase inhibitors, anthracyclines and ion-channel trafficking inhibitors, over 4 days, combining field potential recordings with impedance readouts. (3) [High-throughput] chronic and longitudinal MEA assays "have been validated for both acute and delayed cardiotoxicity" (this study is the example). (4) [High-throughput] it is an example of multi-parameter feature sets encompassing beat rate, beat-to-beat variability, conduction properties and event-based descriptors (cited together with Matsuda et al., 2025).

## Blinova et al. (2018)

Local file: `raw/blinova-2018-multisite.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/blinova-2018-multisite.md`. Status: checked (Methods, Results, Discussion; Figures 1, 2, 5–7 viewed).

Claims: (1) [Intro] the study is a source for FPD and related metrics being integral to the CiPA initiative (cited with Park et al., 2025). (2) [FPDc] it was a "blinded multisite CiPA study" that applied Fridericia correction to all 28 reference drugs across ten sites. (3) [FPDc] it states that the Fridericia formula was not thoroughly validated for hiPSC-CMs, and this is described as a recognised methodological gap. (4) [FPDc] spontaneous rates of hiPSC-CMs are "accepted at 20–90 BPM" in this study (inclusion criterion for baseline beating rate). (5) [Limitations] it is cited only as pre-2022 background for conventions and datasets that later studies build on, not as one of the post-2022 developments reviewed. Pointers: Fridericia statement in the Data Analysis section of the Experimental Procedures; the 20–90 bpm criterion in the same section; ten sites and 28 drugs in the Summary.

## Botti et al. (2026)

Local file: `raw/botti-2026-insilico.pdf` (+ peer review history); page `wiki/papers/botti-2026-insilico.md`. Status: checked against the Europe PMC full text; compare with the PDF.

Claims: (1) [Model complexity] it is a two-dimensional bidomain model of a six-well MEA with phenotype-specific ventricular-like and atrial-like ionic models. (2) it simulated how the atrial-like fraction changes conduction velocity and repolarization. (3) it reproduced Brugada-like field potentials. (4) it compared simulated effects of four ion-channel blockers (mexiletine, nifedipine, dofetilide, E-4031) with previously published MEA data, without new experiments and with a fixed 30 % atrial-like fraction in the drug simulations. (5) it is a monolayer model and does not address the 3D question directly. (6) [Limitations] it is an example of simulation-based evidence. (7) The reference year is 2026 (issue 604(19), October 2026; online 11 May 2025).

## Clements et al. (2025)

(3) [Maturation] it is an industry-led proposal (Axion BioSystems' "AIMS \#CM01", presented as a poster at ISSCR 2025) defining minimum well-acceptance criteria for spontaneous-beating hiPSC-ventricular-CM MEA assays: spike amplitude ≥ 0.5 mV and synchronized beating on at least half of the electrodes, a spontaneous beat period of 750–3000 ms, and FPDc of 200–700 ms. Pointers: the "AIMS \#CM01: Proposed standards" box; "Presented at ISSCR 2025, Hong Kong, June 11, 2025". ## Correia et al. (2024)

Local files: `raw/correia-2024-acc2.pdf` (+ supplementary); page `wiki/papers/correia-2024-acc2.md`. Status: from the wiki page.

Claims: (1) [Timeline] ACC2-inhibitor maturation medium improves in vitro-to-in vivo translatability (2024). (2) [Maturation] they developed a maturation medium, a galactose- and fatty-acid-based formulation with a triiodothyronine/IGF-1/dexamethasone cocktail, augmented with a small-molecule acetyl-CoA carboxylase 2 (ACC2) inhibitor. (3) it improved patch-clamp-defined electrophysiological maturity (a more hyperpolarized maximum diastolic potential, faster action potential upstroke velocity, and a six-fold increase in inward-rectifier K⁺ current) alongside metabolic and structural maturation. (4) an in vitro-to-in vivo pharmacokinetic correlation for a drug target across 21 compounds was achieved only in matured cells (Pearson r = 0.85, R² = 0.72, p < 0.0001, spanning about four orders of magnitude). (5) standard-medium cells largely failed to respond to the same compounds at all, so no correlation could be computed. (6) therefore maturation status gates translational validity. (7) [Conclusion] in vitro-to-in vivo translatability depends on maturation status. Pointer: Figure 7F for the correlation.

## Ernault et al. (2024)

Local file: `raw/ernault-2024-interpretation.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/ernault-2024-interpretation.md`. Status: from the wiki page.

Claims: (1) [Timeline, Table 2, Figure 2] T-wave peak equals APD90 for biphasic T waves, in NRVM. (2) [Fiducial] using simultaneous MEA and sharp-microelectrode recordings from neonatal rat ventricular myocyte (NRVM) monolayers, the peak time of the T wave in FPs correlates strongly with APD90, but only when the T wave displays a biphasic pattern. (3) the authors report that morphology in only a minority of recorded monolayers. (4) [Fiducial, our inference] the T-wave peak, when biphasic, is better anchored to APD90 than a baseline-return convention, which extends into the repolarization tail; the two definitions measure different quantities. (5) [Morphology] together with Weiser-Bitoun et al., it is cited for post-2022 studies increasingly reporting morphology-dependent exclusion criteria or analysis branches. (6) [Limitations] example of evidence in rat cells.

## Fetterman et al. (2024)

Local file: `raw/fetterman-2024-maturation.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/fetterman-2024-maturation.md`. Status: from the wiki page.

Claims: (1) [Timeline] functional, metabolic and transcriptional maturation shown independent. (2) [Maturation] they systematically varied nine cardiomyocyte culture-medium formulations. (3) functional (electrophysiology and excitation-contraction coupling), metabolic and transcriptional maturation are induced largely independently by different medium components. (4) interventions that improved action potential kinetics and Na⁺ current density had no effect on mitochondrial respiration, and vice versa. (5) therefore maturation is not a single scalar quantity and any MEA-derived reference value could characterise only the electrophysiological axis. (6) [Conclusion] the same independence is the reason a benchmark would be scoped to the electrophysiological axis. (7) [Limitations] example of a non-MEA modality.

## Guerrelli et al. (2024)

Local file: `raw/guerrelli-2024-hipscm.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/guerrelli-2024-hipscm.md`. Status: from the wiki page.

Claims: (1) [Timeline] temporal and spatial confounders quantified. (2) [FPDc] one study reported that the beating rate increases by more than 22 % and FPD decreases by about 8 % during a 20-minute recording equilibration period. (3) [High-throughput] the same numbers more precisely: beating rate +22.6 % and FPD −7.7 % over a 20-minute equilibration window. (4) [High-throughput] from 2 to 14 days in culture the beating rate decreases while FPD lengthens substantially. (5) [Maturation] FPD lengthens by about 257 ms and beating rate declines over days 2–14 in culture. (6) [Maturation] KCNJ2 (I_K1-encoding) gene expression rises 27.5-fold over 30 days. (7) [Maturation, our interpretation] this drift is consistent with ongoing maturation and rate correction alone does not address it. (8) [High-throughput] the findings highlight the need to standardise recording time points and culture age.

## Ismaili et al. (2023)

## Mäki (2023, version v3)

Local file: `raw/maki-2023-opinion.html`; page `wiki/papers/maki-2023-opinion.md`; also `wiki/critical-assessment-maki-claims.md`. Status: the 3D limitation sentence and the inversion statements checked against the HTML; others from the wiki page. This is the author's own opinion paper.

Claims: (1) [Abstract, Motivation, Scope] it is used as the evaluative framework. (2) [Motivation] it challenged the physiological validity of commonly used FP annotation conventions, arguing that the widely adopted peak-to-peak FPD definition is often mismatched to the underlying electrophysiology. (3) it argues that the first positive FP peak in a propagating hiPSC-CM monolayer may reflect depolarization of neighbouring cells rather than the locally recorded cardiomyocyte. (4) it argues that FPD end should be measured to the return of the signal to baseline rather than the repolarization peak. (5) [Fiducial] it describes an algorithm: beat-aligned segmentation, averaging across beats to reduce noise, threshold-based identification of depolarization onset relative to FP amplitude, FPD end as return of the signal toward baseline. (6) [Morphology] morphology-aware handling of pacemaker-like signals and waveform inversion for "only high peak" cases, internally validated in that work but not independently replicated. (7) [Model complexity] its caution that analysis frameworks optimised for 2D cultures may not be directly transferable to 3D systems (its Limitations section says the analysis "might not be as easily … extrapolated to the 3D model"). (8) [Table 1, Table 2, Figure 2] DatAnalyzer (Python): FPD from depolarization onset to baseline return; the table also characterises the "peak-to-peak" convention as common and criticised in this paper. (9) [Declarations, References] the first version was posted on Zenodo in 2023; the text assessed is v3, published 23 June 2026 (the version list of the record zenodo.org/records/20807245 reads "Version v3, 10.5281/zenodo.20807245, Jun 23, 2026"; the "Updated 2025-08-07" date on the record page is a separate metadata field and not the publication date). The reference entry keeps the year 2023 of the original version and states "(Version v3, published 23 June 2026)"; the Declarations version note says the same.

## Claims that rest on the author's own synthesis or are not cited

These are statements in `main.md` that no single reference supports, or that are generalisations from one or two studies. Judge them yourself. Each item gives the section, the wording in `main.md`, what it is based on (source claims above, by source name), and how strong the support is in my reading: "none" means no source in the reference list supports it, "thin" means one or two studies, "inference" means a reasoned step from sources that is the author's own. Items 1–4 include the four methodological gaps of the Abstract and Conclusion.

1. [Abstract, Motivation] "the methods used to analyse FP signals have emerged as a primary source of inter-study variability" and, in bold, "signal analysis methods—particularly the definition of fiducial points in field potential (FP) waveforms—have emerged as a major source of variability and a limiting factor for inter-study comparability" (you marked this with "needs citation(s)"). Based on: Mäki (2023) as the argument; Lee et al. (2024), whose FPD correlated minimally with APD because of small T waves; Ernault et al. (2024); the spread of conventions in Table 2. No reviewed study measures how much analysis methods contribute to inter-study variability, and Blinova et al. (2018) found site variability smaller than cell-line and concentration effects (without testing analysis methods). Support: none for "major/primary source" as stated; consider softening or citing.
2. [Abstract, Conclusion] Gap 1: "FPD rate correction remains unsolved: the Fridericia formula, borrowed from clinical electrocardiography, lacks formal validation for the beating rate range and rate–FPD relationship characteristic of hiPSC-CM cultures. Developing a cell-type–specific correction formula — ideally derived from large multi-laboratory datasets — would substantially improve the reliability of FPDc as a pharmacological endpoint." Based on: Blinova et al. (2018) (stated as not thoroughly validated, rates accepted at 20–90 BPM); Clements et al. (2025) (20–80 BPM and an FPDc window); Guerrelli et al. (2024) (drift during equilibration); Pramudito et al. (2026), Park et al. (2026) and Lee et al. (2024) (correction taken as given or absent). Not based on any study that tested an alternative correction (none reviewed). "Borrowed from clinical electrocardiography" and "would substantially improve" are uncited. Support: thin for the validation gap, none for the recommendation (it is an opinion).
3. [Abstract, Conclusion] Gap 2: "waveform morphology classification is still largely informal: studies describe exclusion rules and morphology-specific handling in qualitative terms rather than through validated shared criteria. Standardised morphology assessment analogous to existing beat acceptance thresholds (amplitude, rate, beat regularity) would reduce between-study variability at the data collection stage." Based on: Mäki (2023) (three morphologies, own method), Ernault et al. (2024) (biphasic T waves a minority), Weiser-Bitoun et al. (2026) (inverted T peaks), Lee et al. (2024) (small T waves). Quantitative acceptance criteria do exist (Blinova et al., 2018; Clements et al., 2025: amplitude, synchrony, beat period, FPDc) but they concern well acceptance, not waveform classification, and the Conclusion does not cite them. No source says that morphology handling is "informal". Support: thin; the statement is an inference from a small set of papers.
4. [Abstract, Conclusion] Gap 3: "the transfer of 2D-calibrated analysis frameworks to 3D models and ultra-high-density platforms proceeds empirically: dedicated validation of fiducial definitions and conduction metrics in 3D cardiac tissues and spatially resolved MEA recordings is needed". Based on: Hwang et al. (2023) (3D variability), Maurissen et al. (2024) (3D tissue sheets needed for TdP-like waveforms), Matsuda et al. (2025), Mäki (2023)'s own limitation sentence, Botti et al. (2026) (2D model only). No study tested the transfer of fiducial definitions to 3D, so "proceeds empirically" and "is needed" are inferences. Support: thin. Gap 4: "hiPSC-CM maturation status lacks MEA-derived reference values: no validated benchmark currently allows a laboratory to report how mature its preparation was at the time of recording". Based on: Guerrelli et al. (2024), Fetterman et al. (2024), Correia et al. (2024), Scheurer et al. (2025), Clements et al. (2025) (QC, not staging), and the targeted search of 2 July 2026 recorded in `wiki/hipsc-cm-maturation.md`. An absence claim, as strong as that single search; it is not independently reproducible.
5. [Open pipelines, Conclusion] "Together, these platforms point toward reproducibility, transparency, and explicit reporting of analysis parameters, enabling investigators to critically assess how electrophysiological features are derived rather than relying on opaque vendor defaults." Based on: the five open platforms in Table 1 (Dunham, Lee, Weiser-Bitoun, Kabanov, Mäki). Counter-evidence in the paper itself: Park et al. (2025) and Park et al. (2026) used the vendor tool; there are no adoption data. Support: inference from five tools. The PhysioMEA wording is settled: `main.md` says its software, source code and documentation are freely available (written in MATLAB, also distributed as a standalone executable) instead of calling it "open-source MATLAB".
6. [Fiducial, Morphology] "the conventions differ" and "FPD values are therefore not directly comparable across tools without explicit knowledge of the underlying convention". Based on: Table 1 and Table 2 (Dunham, Lee, Weiser-Bitoun, Mäki). No study compared tools or conventions on the same data. Support: inference from the definitions.
7.
