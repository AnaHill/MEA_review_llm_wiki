# Claims in main.md, by source

A checklist for verifying the paper against its sources. Each source has one paragraph listing every claim that `main.md` attributes to it, with the section in brackets. Compiled 2026-10-18 and verified against the source documents on 2026-10-24 (see "Corrections made in the 2026-10-24 verification" at the end); regenerate or update it if `main.md` changes. Verify each claim in the local file named under the source (all files are in `raw/`; page and figure pointers are given where known). "Checked" below means that the claims were compared with the full text of the source (the date is given in each status line); where the check found a mismatch, the wording in `main.md` was corrected and the correction is described under the source. Even so, the verification was done by reading and searching the extracted text of the PDFs, and figures were viewed only where stated, so your own check is still needed for numbers, figure readings and nuances of wording.

Sections cited in brackets: Intro, Motivation, Scope, Timeline (Figure 1), Open pipelines (Transition section), Table 1, Fiducial (definitions section), Table 2, FPDc (rate correction), Maturation, Morphology (incl. Figure 2), Model complexity, High-throughput, Limitations, Conclusion.

## Altrocchi et al. (2023)

Local file: `raw/altrocchi-2023-chronic.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/altrocchi-2023-chronic.md`. Status: checked against the full text 2026-10-24.

Claims: (1) [FPDc] the study reports rate-corrected FPD with the Fridericia formula (listed among the studies that use it). (2) [High-throughput] it used 48-well MEA plates to evaluate acute (<2 h) and delayed (≥24 h) electrophysiological and cytotoxic effects of reference compounds, including tyrosine-kinase inhibitors, an anthracycline (doxorubicin) and ion-channel trafficking inhibitors, over 4 days, combining field potential recordings with impedance readouts. (3) [High-throughput] chronic and longitudinal MEA assays "have been validated for both acute and delayed cardiotoxicity" (this study is the example). (4) [High-throughput] it is one of the two examples (with Matsuda et al., 2025) of multi-parameter feature sets encompassing beat rate, beat-to-beat variability, conduction properties and event-based descriptors. (5) [Intro] cited, with Blinova et al. (2018) and Park et al. (2025), for MEA recordings of hiPSC-CMs being widely used in cardiac safety pharmacology, drug discovery and disease modelling.

Check 2026-10-24: (1) confirmed, the paper writes "Fredericia correction formula, FPDc (Blinova et al., 2018)" (p. 5). (2) confirmed: abstract p. 1 (acute <2 h, delayed ≥24 h, 48-well plates, recordings for 4 days), Methods p. 3 (Axion Cytoview M768-tMEA-48B, up to 96 h); doxorubicin is the only anthracycline (BMS-986094 is the other direct cardiotoxic drug), so the text was changed from "anthracyclines" to "an anthracycline (doxorubicin)". (3) the paper says it "developed and validated a functional assay" with 13 reference compounds (p. 3). (4) partly: the study itself reports beating rate, FP amplitude, FPD/FPDc, impedance and arrhythmic events; beat-to-beat variability and conduction properties come from Matsuda et al. (2025), not from this study.

## Blinova et al. (2018)

Local file: `raw/blinova-2018-multisite.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/blinova-2018-multisite.md`. Status: checked (Methods, Results, Discussion; Figures 1, 2, 5–7 viewed) 2026-10-18.

Claims: (1) [Intro] cited, with Altrocchi et al. (2023) and Park et al. (2025), for MEA recordings of hiPSC-CMs being widely used in cardiac safety pharmacology, drug discovery and disease modelling, and, with Park et al. (2) [FPDc] it was a "blinded multisite CiPA study" that applied Fridericia correction to all 28 reference drugs across ten sites. ## Correia et al. (2024)

Local files: `raw/correia-2024-acc2.pdf` (+ supplementary); page `wiki/papers/correia-2024-acc2.md`. Status: checked against the full text and Figure 7F 2026-10-24.

Claims: (1) [Timeline] ACC2-inhibitor maturation medium improves in vitro-to-in vivo translatability (2024). (2) [Maturation] they developed a maturation medium, a galactose- and fatty-acid-based formulation with a triiodothyronine/IGF-1/dexamethasone cocktail, augmented with a small-molecule acetyl-CoA carboxylase 2 (ACC2) inhibitor. (3) it improved patch-clamp-defined electrophysiological maturity (a more hyperpolarized maximum diastolic potential, faster action potential upstroke velocity, and a six-fold increase in inward-rectifier K⁺ current) alongside metabolic and structural maturation. (4) an in vitro-to-in vivo target-engagement correlation (in vitro IC50 against in vivo IC50 in rat plasma) across 21 inhibitors of one drug target, BCKDK, was achieved only in matured cells (Pearson r = 0.85, R² = 0.72, p < 0.0001, data spanning roughly three orders of magnitude). (5) standard-medium cells showed no significant response to BCKDK inhibition, so no correlation could be computed. (6) therefore, for this target, maturation status can gate translational validity. (7) [Conclusion] for one drug target, in vitro-to-in vivo correlation was obtained only in matured cells.

Check 2026-10-24: (2) p. 3, medium with oleic and palmitic acid, 10 mM galactose, 1 µM dexamethasone, 100 nM T3, 100 ng/mL IGF1 and 5 µM ACC2 inhibitor (CP640186). (3) p. 14: maximum diastolic potential −79.2 ± 4 vs −74.3 ± 8.2 mV, dV/dt max 68.6 ± 43.5 vs 23.4 ± 25.6 V/s; p. 16: IK1 6-fold (−27.3 vs −4.1 pA/pF). (4) Figure 7F (p. 22) reads r 0.85, R² 0.72, p < 0.0001 and 21 BCKDK inhibitors; the x-axis spans four decades but the data span about three, so the earlier "four orders of magnitude" was changed to "roughly three"; "pharmacokinetic" was changed to "target-engagement". (5) p. 23: "not possible to obtain using standard medium"; BCAT inhibitors responded in both media, so the statement is restricted to BCKDK inhibition. (6)(7) one drug target only, so the earlier wording that translatability "itself depends on maturation status" was restricted.

## Ernault et al. (2024)

Local file: `raw/ernault-2024-interpretation.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/ernault-2024-interpretation.md`. Status: checked against the abstract and Results 2026-10-24.

Claims: (1) [Timeline, Table 2, Figure 2] T-wave peak equals APD90 for biphasic T waves, in NRVM. (2) [Fiducial] using simultaneous MEA and sharp-microelectrode recordings from neonatal rat ventricular myocyte (NRVM) monolayers, the peak time of the T wave in FPs correlates strongly with APD90, but only when the T wave displays a biphasic pattern. ## Fetterman et al. (2024)

Local file: `raw/fetterman-2024-maturation.pdf`; page `wiki/papers/fetterman-2024-maturation.md`. Status: checked against the full text 2026-10-24.

Claims: (1) [Timeline] functional, metabolic and transcriptional maturation shown independent. (2) [Maturation] they systematically added and removed individual components of a defined maturation medium and compared nine medium formulations by RNA sequencing. (3) functional (electrophysiology and excitation-contraction coupling), metabolic and transcriptional maturation are induced largely independently by different medium components; for example, removing fatty acids lowered mitochondrial respiration without changing Ca²⁺ dynamics. (4) therefore maturation is not a single scalar quantity and any MEA-derived reference value could characterise only the electrophysiological axis. (5) [Conclusion] the same independence is the reason a benchmark would be scoped to the electrophysiological axis. (6) [Limitations] example of a non-MEA modality.

Check 2026-10-24: (3) summary p. 2 ("sub-specified into electrophysiological/EC coupling, metabolism, and gene expression and that induction of these attributes is largely independent"); p. 7 ("Fatty acids contribute to metabolism but not viability, Ca²⁺ dynamics, impedance, or Nav current"). (2) "nine medium formulations" applies to the RNA-seq comparison (Figure 6, p. 10); the main design is single-addition and single-subtraction experiments in minimal formulations (Figure 2, p. 6), so the earlier wording "systematically varied nine formulations" and the sentence about action potential kinetics and Na⁺ current having "no effect on mitochondrial respiration, and vice versa" were replaced by the verified example.

## Mäki (2023, version v3)

Local file: `raw/maki-2023-opinion.html`; page `wiki/papers/maki-2023-opinion.md`; also `wiki/critical-assessment-maki-claims.md`. Status: claims 2–6 and 7 checked against the HTML 2026-10-24 (propagating monolayer, neighbouring cells, 10 % thresholds, Limitations sentence); the rest from the wiki page. This is the author's own opinion paper.

Claims: (1) [Abstract, Motivation, Scope, Timeline] it is used as the evaluative framework; the Figure 1 timeline lists "DatAnalyzer / Mäki opinion paper — morphology-aware FPD" under 2023. (2) [Motivation] it challenged the physiological validity of commonly used FP annotation conventions, arguing that the widely adopted peak-to-peak FPD definition is often mismatched to the underlying electrophysiology. (3) it argues that the first positive FP peak in a propagating hiPSC-CM monolayer may reflect depolarization of neighbouring cells rather than the locally recorded cardiomyocyte. (4) it argues that FPD end should be measured to the return of the signal to baseline rather than the repolarization peak. (5) [Fiducial] it describes an algorithm: beat-aligned segmentation, averaging across beats to reduce noise, threshold-based identification of depolarization onset relative to FP amplitude, FPD end as return of the signal toward baseline. (6) [Morphology] morphology-aware handling of pacemaker-like signals and waveform inversion for "only high peak" cases, internally validated in that work but not independently replicated. (7) [Model complexity] its caution that analysis frameworks optimised for 2D cultures may not be directly transferable to 3D systems (its Limitations section says the analysis "might not be as easily … extrapolated to the 3D model"). (8) [Table 1, Table 2, Figure 2] DatAnalyzer (Python): FPD from depolarization onset to baseline return; the merged peak-to-peak row of Table 2 also characterises that convention as common and criticised in this paper; Figure 2 and the Morphology paragraph also cite it for the baseline-return end. (9) [Declarations, References] the first version was posted on Zenodo in 2023; the text assessed is v3, published 23 June 2026 (the version list of the record zenodo.org/records/20807245 reads "Version v3, 10.5281/zenodo.20807245, Jun 23, 2026"; the "Updated 2025-08-07" date on the record page is a separate metadata field and not the publication date). The reference entry keeps the year 2023 of the original version and states "(Version v3, published 23 June 2026)"; the Declarations version note says the same. (10) [Limitations] it is the author's own, non-peer-reviewed opinion paper; its inversion approach is not independently replicated; its FPD-end convention is one of several. (11) [Declarations] competing-interest statement: the author wrote this paper and the DatAnalyzer tool.

## Park et al. (2026)

Local files: `raw/park-2026-genotype-ml.pdf` (+ supplementary methods and Data set S1); page `wiki/papers/park-2026-genotype-ml.md`. Status: checked (full text, supplementary, all eight figures) 2026-10-18.
