b# Claims in main.md, by source

A checklist for verifying the paper against its sources. Each source has a list of every claim that `main.md` attributes to it, with the section in brackets; tick `[x]` when you have checked a claim against the source. The last section is the register of the author's own inferences, which `main.md` does not mark in the text; its reading guide defines the **Support** vocabulary (`sourced`, `thin`, `inference`, `opinion`, `none`, `inherited`) that says where each unsourced statement's warrant comes from, and `scripts/check.py` warns about a Support line that falls outside it. Verify each claim in the local file named under the source (all files are in `raw/`; page and figure pointers are given where known). The wiki pages were checked against the source texts on 2026-10-24 and later; figures were viewed only where stated, so numbers, figure readings and nuances of wording still need your own check.

This version was written from scratch on 2026-11-01 from the current `main.md`; ticks set before that date were kept where the claim is unchanged in substance. The history of earlier wordings and corrections is in `wiki/log.md` and git. Run `python -I scripts/claim_quotes.py --write` to list the current `main.md` sentence under each claim (the generated `- >` lines are rewritten on every run; a dry run without `--write` changes nothing).

Sections cited in brackets: Abstract, Highlights, Intro, Motivation, Scope, Timeline (Figure 1), Open pipelines, Table 1, Fiducial, Table 2, FPDc (rate correction), Maturation, Morphology (incl. Figure 2), 3D (section "Three-dimensional cardiomyocyte models"), High-throughput (section "High-throughput and long-term assays"), Outlook (Discussion and future outlook), Limitations, Conclusion, Declarations.

## ✅ Register of the author's own inferences

These are statements in `main.md` that no single reference supports, generalisations from one or two studies, and the author's hypotheses. `main.md` does not mark them in the text, so this list is their only record; every new unsourced inference in `main.md` needs an item here. Tick `[x]` when you have decided that you stand behind the wording.

Reading guide for each item: **bold** = the item's topic and where it sits in `main.md`; "quoted" = the wording in `main.md`; **Based on** = the sources behind it; **Origin** = whose idea it was and when, where that is worth recording; **Support** = my reading of where the statement's warrant comes from, one of:

- `sourced` — a source states this directly, and the evidence behind it carries the wording used.
- `thin` — a source states it, but on one or two studies, so the risk is that it does not generalise.
- `inference` — no source states it, but it follows by a reasoned step from what sources do state. The risk is in the step, not in the sources: the item can fail although every source is right.
- `opinion` — a judgement or a preference of the author, not a claim about the world that a source could settle.
- `none` — no source supports it at all: a hypothesis or an idea of the author's own, and `main.md` says so where it appears.
- `inherited` — the item summarises other items and takes their support; used by item 0 only.

These are not points on one scale. `thin` is sourced but narrow; `inference` is not sourced as such but derived; they fail in different ways and need different fixes, a better study for the first and a better argument for the second. Every Support line carries at least one of these six values, and `scripts/check.py` warns about a line that carries none or that introduces a seventh; the vocabulary is fixed here, so add a value only by editing this list. A value may be followed by a qualifier in plain words, and where parts of one item have different support, each part is named with its own value ("`sourced` for the values; the comparison is ours"). An absence claim ("no study examined here ...") is only as strong as the search behind it, so its Support names that search beside the value.
