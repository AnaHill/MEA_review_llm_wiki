# hiPSC-CM maturation status as a confound for MEA-derived metrics

## Why this matters
hiPSC-CMs are widely described as resembling fetal rather than adult cardiomyocytes (Correia et al. 2024; Scheurer et al. 2025). They express the funny current (If) and occasionally the T-type Ca²⁺ current, which are absent from adult human working myocardium (although the presence of ICa,T is not necessarily a sign of immaturity); whether ventricular-, atrial- and nodal-like cells form distinct subpopulations within one preparation is debated (Ismaili et al. 2023), but patch-clamp data show them in the lines of Park et al. (2026). Every MEA metric discussed elsewhere in this wiki — FPD, its rate correction, waveform morphology, conduction velocity — is measured on cells whose maturation state is rarely quantified, only assumed or loosely controlled via culture duration.

This creates two linked problems this review otherwise treats separately:
1. **Inter-study comparability.** Two labs reporting different FPDc values for the same compound may simply differ in how mature their cultures are, not in methodology.
2. **Clinical translation.** MEA endpoints are ultimately meant to stand in for adult human myocardial electrophysiology; the less mature the model, the less licensed that inference is. Correia et al. (2024) give this concrete empirical weight outside the MEA context, for one drug target: with their ACC2-inhibitor maturation medium, in vitro IC50 values of 21 BCKDK inhibitors correlated with in vivo rat plasma IC50 values (a target-engagement correlation, r = 0.85), whereas standard-medium cells largely failed to respond to BCKDK inhibition, so no correlation could be computed. For this target, maturation status was the difference between a usable and an unusable in vitro-to-in vivo correlation.

## What maturation is not
Fetterman et al. (2024) is a clear demonstration that "maturation" is not one thing. By adding and removing individual components of a defined medium, and comparing nine medium formulations by RNA-seq, they found that **functional (electrophysiology/EC-coupling), metabolic, and transcriptional maturation are induced largely independently** by different medium components — for example, removing fatty acids lowered mitochondrial respiration without changing Ca²⁺ dynamics (summary, p. 2; p. 7). A single scalar "maturation score" derived from any one modality — including MEA — could therefore describe only one compartment of maturity (our inference), not the whole phenotype.

This directly bears on the colleague-suggested idea of deriving MEA-based reference values for maturation staging: such a metric would be a legitimate and useful reference for the *electrophysiological* axis specifically, but would need to be explicitly scoped as such rather than presented as a general maturity index.

## What partial evidence already exists
- **Culture-age effects on baseline electrophysiology are already quantified in MEA terms.** Guerrelli et al. (2024) found FPD lengthens by ~257 ms and beating rate falls over days 2–14 in culture, alongside a 27.5-fold increase in KCNJ2 (I_K1) expression over 30 days — i.e., a maturation-associated electrophysiological drift that current FPDc practice does not control for or report against a maturity axis.
- **Patch-clamp values for more mature hiPSC-CMs exist, but not for MEA.** Fetterman et al. (2024) report, for their complete maturation medium versus a control medium, a maximum diastolic potential of −68.1 versus −51.7 mV and an AP upstroke velocity of 30.2 versus 7.3 mV/ms; Correia et al. (2024) report −79.2 versus −74.3 mV and a six-fold higher IK1, which they compare with adult human and rabbit ventricular cells (p. 24). These are intracellular, not extracellular FP, measurements, so they cannot be applied to MEA data without a validated conversion.
- **MEA field-potential parameters separate maturation conditions.** Li et al. (2025) found that conduction velocity, spike amplitude and spike slope rise from B27-cultured to maturation-medium iPSC-CMs (CV 12.5 cm/s in B27, 22.3 in MM, 27.8 with nanopatterning and electrostimulation; spike amplitude 3.2 vs 10.4 vs 13.6 mV), in the same direction as patch-clamp markers measured in other cells (more negative RMP, higher Vmax, INa and IK1). B27 cultures also stopped beating with 1 µM verapamil and showed only minor FPDc prolongation with E-4031. The parameters are reported as group means; the paper proposes no thresholds or reference values and does not compare the FP values with adult data.
- **Machine learning on extracellular waveforms has traced electrical maturation, outside the MEA-plate format.** Lin et al. (2023) recorded field potentials from stretchable mesh electronics embedded in 3D hiPSC-CM microtissues (with or without hiPSC-derived endothelial cells) over days 15–37 and applied unsupervised pseudotime inference (PHATE + Slingshot) to the whole spike waveforms. Amplitude and dV/dt rose and the depolarization spike width fell along the inferred trajectory, and cocultures lay further along it at every time point. "Maturation" here rests on recording day and feature direction: there is no repolarization feature, no external maturity measure on the same cultures (scRNA-seq was done on separate cultures), no reference values, and one cell line.
- **No validated MEA-only maturation reference values or index were found.** A first check on 2026-07-02 used web searches (PubMed/bioRxiv/Scopus scope) without recorded queries. A documented search on 2026-10-30 (PubMed queries with record counts, citation chasing, web searches including bioRxiv; no date limit; Scopus not searched) found no work proposing or validating MEA reference values, thresholds or a classifier for maturation; the record, candidates and selection are in docs/maturation-mea-search.md. The closest development is an industry/consortium effort (Axion Biosystems "AIMS \#CM01", presented at ISSCR 2025) proposing minimum well-acceptance criteria for spontaneous-beating hiPSC-ventricular-CM MEA assays (beat rate, spike amplitude, FPD, syncytial synchronization) — but this targets assay quality control, not maturation staging specifically, and has not yet appeared as a peer-reviewed, citable methods paper.
- **A validated maturity classifier already exists for an adjacent, non-MEA modality.** Scheurer et al. (2025) trained an interpretable SVM/SHAP classifier on video-based beating features alone, reaching 99.5% accuracy in separating immature (day 21) from lipid-medium-matured (day 42) cultures (a binary classifier; a less mature day-42 group in another medium gave a heterogeneous distribution between the two clusters). Its "mature" (maturation medium) and "less mature" (B27) labels rest on Li et al. (2025) from the same group, which used the same two media and reported MEA differences between them (above); so the culture conditions the video classifier separates already have MEA field-potential data, but no classifier has been trained on those FP features (our reading). The paper also cites prior work (Hayakawa et al., 2014; [[wiki/papers/hayakawa-2014-motion-fp.md]]) in which MEA field potentials and image-based motion were recorded simultaneously and FPD and contraction–relaxation duration were linearly related across drug concentrations (with a different slope for each drug, non-arrhythmic conditions). That paper does not study maturation, so it links FPD to contraction, not to the maturation process the optical classifier detects; our reading is that an FPD-based equivalent of Scheurer's classifier has not, to our knowledge, been built (a targeted search found none, see above).

## The gap
No study reviewed here proposes or validates MEA-derived reference values that stage hiPSC-CM maturation status on the electrophysiological axis specifically, in a form that would let independent labs report "how mature" their preparation was at time of recording — analogous to how FPDc lets them report "how rate-corrected" a measurement is. Given Fetterman et al. (2024)'s finding that maturation compartments are independent, any such index should be explicitly scoped to the electrophysiological/FP-observable axis rather than framed as a general maturity score. Scheurer et al. (2025) sharpens this from an absence-of-evidence gap to a concrete, addressable one: the methodology (interpretable ML on beat-derived features), the validation approach (cross-validated accuracy + SHAP), the physiological link (FPD related to contraction duration within a drug, in non-arrhythmic conditions; Hayakawa et al., 2014) and MEA field-potential data for the very culture conditions Scheurer's classifier separates (Li et al., 2025) all already exist in the literature — they simply have not been assembled into an MEA-native maturity classifier analogous to Scheurer's video-based one. The nearest combination is Lin et al. (2023): unsupervised machine learning on extracellular waveforms that orders recordings along an electrical maturation trajectory. It leaves the gap open in four respects: it uses tissue-embedded electronics in 3D microtissues rather than MEA plates and 2D monolayers, it has depolarization features only (no FPD), it has no external maturity measure on the same cultures and no reference values, and it uses one cell line.
