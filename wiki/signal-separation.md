# Separating cell, tissue and device contributions to the field potential

Concept page, written 2026-11-03 from the wiki paper pages and the "Separating the recording system from the cellular signal" part of the Discussion and future outlook in main.md (Highlight 6). It collects what the sources say about how much of a recorded field potential (FP) comes from the cells, the surrounding tissue and the recording device, and keeps those statements apart from the author's hypothesis. The sensor-fusion side is on [[wiki/multimodal-measurement.md]].

## Hypothesis (ours, not from the sources)
- **Inverse approach (register item 26):** estimate the device contribution, and as far as possible the tissue contribution, from the measured signal by reverse engineering (for example system identification), so that the remaining signal resembles the cellular AP more closely. Paired FP and electroporation signals (Lee et al., 2024) and further simultaneous modalities could give training and validation data. A calibrated model of cells, tissue and device would be a digital twin of the recording. Support: `none` for the approach itself; its building blocks are the sourced statements above.
- **Impedance as an attachment marker (register item 27):** an impedance channel could flag FP changes caused by altered attachment rather than by the AP. Support: mechanism from Karami (distance sensitivity) and the observation from Altrocchi (impedance falls with poorer attachment); the use to flag FP changes is untested.
- **Separated signal as machine-learning input (register item 25):** Lin et al. (2023) is the only sourced part, and it used raw waveforms, not separated ones.

## Observations (ours)
- **Four main.md sentences rest on Karami et al. (2026) alone:** the opening statement, the equivalent circuit, the superposition behind the ill-posed point, and the 1 nm impedance sensitivity. Karami is a narrative review with several citation slips (see its page). These four statements are textbook-level, and the page names the primary sources Karami cites for them (Fromherz et al., 1991; Spira and Hai, 2013; Giaever and Keese, 1991), none of which is in raw/. Citing one primary source next to Karami would make the device-separation part less dependent on a single secondary review.
- **Caution on Ernault for "neighbouring cells":** the Ernault page records the neighbouring-cell current for the simulated LEAP, not for the ordinary FP. The general statement that the FP depends on currents from surrounding cells is Karami's superposition point. Check Ernault claim (5) in docs/claims-by-source.md against the PDF.
- **Lee et al. (2024) give their own explanation:** they attribute the weak FPD–APD correlation to noise and small T waves, that is, partly to the recording rather than only to a mismatch between FP and AP. This fits the separation argument, but it is the authors' measurement explanation, not evidence for it.
- No source attempts the inverse problem on MEA data from hiPSC-CMs. Botti's model goes only in the forward direction, and Lee 2026 states the hardware effect without quantifying it.
