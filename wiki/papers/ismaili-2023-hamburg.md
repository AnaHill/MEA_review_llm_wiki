# Human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes as an electrophysiological model: Opportunities and challenges—The Hamburg perspective

**Authors:** Djemail Ismaili, Carl Schulz, Thomas Eschenhagen, Torsten Christ, Arne Hansen, Donatus Mika, Jussi T. Koivumäki, András Horváth  
**Year:** 2023  
**Venue:** Frontiers in Physiology 14:1132165 (published 16 Feb 2023; open access, CC BY)  
**File:** [[raw/ismaili-2023-hamburg.pdf]]  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fphys.2023.1132165

> **Note:** Page rewritten 2026-10-24 after checking the full text. The earlier version said that the paper discusses MEA field potentials, FPD as a QT surrogate and Fridericia rate correction; the paper contains none of these (MEA, FPD and Fridericia are not mentioned). It is a patch-clamp-centred perspective.

## Contribution
A perspective from the Hamburg group on using hiPSC-CMs as an electrophysiological model, organised as questions: which ion currents hiPSC-CMs express ("all aboard or even stowaways?"), how atrial-like cells can be obtained, how specific computational models are, whether automated patch clamp suits hiPSC-CMs, and further topics (LQT1 phenotypes, Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger, PDE4, interpretation of Ca²⁺ currents, dynamic clamp).

## Methodology
Narrative perspective drawing on the group's own experiments and the literature; no new primary data are presented as a study.

## Findings
- hiPSC-CMs express ion channels that are absent from adult human working myocardium: the funny current (If; HCN4 transcripts are high in hiPSC-derived engineered heart tissue and absent in normal human ventricle) and, occasionally, the T-type Ca²⁺ current (ICa,T). The authors caution that the presence of ICa,T is not necessarily an indication of immaturity, because it has been found in adult ventricular myocardium of many species, and that ICa,T can make up 20–30 % of peak Ca²⁺ current in a typical ICa,L test pulse, so ICa,L should be quantified as the dihydropyridine-sensitive current.
- A less negative diastolic potential of hiPSC-CMs is generally assumed to be a marker of immaturity (low IK1), but the authors note that voltage-sensitive-dye measurements cannot resolve diastolic membrane potential.
- On cell populations: a study of 320 hiPSC-CMs found that APD90 followed a single Gaussian distribution, arguing against a truly mixed cell population; the authors note that the method may have lacked the power to detect distinct populations.
- Atrial models: retinoic acid is needed to induce atrial-like action potentials; the authors question how atrial-like the resulting cells are.
- Automated patch clamp: high throughput, and ball-shaped hiPSC-CMs suit it better than adult cardiomyocytes.

## Limitations
Perspective from one group; no systematic review of the literature. The paper does not address MEA recordings, field potential duration or rate correction.

## Relevance to our paper
Cited in main.md for: the broad challenges of hiPSC-CMs as electrophysiological models (ion channel expression, resting membrane potential, cell-subtype identity); the expression of If and, occasionally, ICa,T in hiPSC-CMs; and the debate on whether ventricular-, atrial- and nodal-like cells form distinct subpopulations. It is no longer cited for the use of MEAs in safety pharmacology, because the paper does not discuss MEAs.
