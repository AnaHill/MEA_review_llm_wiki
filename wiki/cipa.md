# CiPA in the reviewed papers

Concept page, written 2026-10-24 from the wiki paper pages. It records how the reviewed papers relate to the Comprehensive in vitro Proarrhythmia Assay (CiPA); it is not an overview of CiPA itself.

- [[wiki/papers/blinova-2018-multisite.md]] — the first large blinded multisite validation of hiPSC-CMs within CiPA: 28 reference compounds (9 low, 11 intermediate, 8 high TdP risk), two commercial cell lines, five platforms (four MEA, one optical), ten sites. Source dataset of Pramudito et al. (2026).
- [[wiki/papers/pramudito-2026-ensemble.md]] — stacking-ensemble ML on two ΔFPDc predictors from the Blinova et al. dataset, 16 held-out compounds.
- [[wiki/papers/park-2025-cardiotoxicity.md]] — eight arrhythmogenic drugs at concentrations from the blinded CiPA pilot study (Millard et al., 2018); the paper says its protocol is in line with the CiPA core protocol and that CiPA facilitated the use of hiPSC-CMs.
- [[wiki/papers/park-2026-genotype-ml.md]] — 28 compounds; its binary high/low labels differ from the CiPA three-class scheme (nine CiPA-intermediate drugs are labelled high; the page notes the labelling is not explained).
- [[wiki/papers/clements-2025-aims-poster.md]] — the AIMS \#CM01 acceptance range was inferred from the CiPA pilot study (Millard et al., 2018).
- [[wiki/papers/matsuda-2025-uhd.md]] — notes that CiPA primarily targets acute toxicity (p. 2); only the seven compounds used on its conventional MEA are called commonly included in CiPA assessments.

## Observation (ours)
Blinova et al. (2018) is the only CiPA study itself; Pramudito et al. (2026) reuses its dataset, and Park et al. (2025) and Clements et al. (2025) take concentrations or acceptance ranges from the CiPA pilot study (Millard et al., 2018).
